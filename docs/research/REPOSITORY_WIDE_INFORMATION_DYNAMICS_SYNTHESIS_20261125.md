## One program, distinct observation channels

**Unifying question:** when a repeated individual can be predicted using its own history, what additional predictive structure remains in other individuals, genealogical records and aggregate observer outputs, and how does the answer depend on observation depth and composition?

The strongest *within-horse* empirical paper concerns realized managed allocation. The other tracks provide complementary, nonexchangeable evidence. They cannot be multiplied or combined as independent replicates. The identity intersections between GPS, anonymous SNP and market panels have not been certified. There is no empirically estimated arrow from genome to allocation to investor judgment or breeding feedback.

## Shared mathematical bridge, not an estimated causal chain

At each *distinct measured endpoint*, consider an own-history predictor B and a conditional auxiliary correction d. Squared-loss gain = 2E[(Y−B)d]−E[d²]. Updating B by u with fixed d changes gain by −2E[du]. In the allocation study this is directly measured on paired horses; other studies provide compatible questions but do not all estimate the same d/u or loss. At a changing sample denominator, population gain also depends on membership. For pedigree/genome, the observation operator (which parent links and markers are present) determines what can be estimated; the actual G and partial A are not verified on the same horses. At the market layer, node and dyad quote residuals are a different projection, not individual-history loss or biological kinship. The general concept is **observation-conditional incremental predictive value**. A future stable-ID panel could test whether residual overlap persists across endpoints; current evidence cannot.

## Cross-endpoint membership counterexample

[New source-linked arithmetic crosswalk](INFORMATION_MEMBERSHIP_NONALIGNMENT_20261125.md): rank gate vs always-family deterioration is amplified by membership (1.735× the same-horse point change, paired interval uncertain); allocation fixed correction vs own-only deterioration is partly offset by membership (38.1% primary). Longer-distance allocation gives a boundary case with sign varying by split. The result compares different estimands, not a pooled effect or formal interaction. Rank gate exposure and signed family contribution mass are retained as separate observation-weight diagnostics.

## Predictive weighting and heterogeneity

[Bounded scalar and two-stratum arithmetic audit](INFORMATION_WEIGHT_BOUNDARY_AND_HETEROGENEITY_20261125.md) separates residual alignment, excess weighting, and energy-weighted heterogeneity (with a clipping penalty). In all three split sensitivities, both fixed and refreshed profiles show a descriptive intermediate-year peak in the extra two-stratum sample-optimal gain. Different yearly cohorts, no new intervals; not a market threshold or general law. Retain earlier-A actual calibration, not these evaluation-outcome-dependent weights.

## Common-cohort refutation check (2026-11-26)

PR95 tested the annual intermediate-year peak on the identical 527 horses/115 recorded sires in 2022–24. All 12 partition/profile/seed point sequences have H2022 > H2023 > H2024; no intermediate peak. Primary zero-versus-positive pre-2022 history group contains only 54 horses (37 sires), so the primary inference is HOLD. Predeclared 0–3 vs 4+ sensitivity has 288 and 239 horses (83 and 84 sires). Main fixed-profile H23−H22 −0.001436815, two-contrast adjusted interval [−0.003536112,−0.000153384]; H23−H24 +0.000136381 [−0.000557192,+0.000936206]. Two of six supported adjusted intervals for H23−H22 are negative; all H23−H24 intervals span zero. Thus the earlier annual-cohort peak is not reproduced on the selected common cohort; it is not a general individual-level law. This does not establish membership as the unique cause, because the nonlinear optimum and selected residual moments differ. Earlier-year scalar transfer has mixed signs and no new intervals. [Results](JAPAN_CONTEXT_TRANSITIONAL_HETEROGENEITY_RESULTS.md).

## Numerical source of the annual/common discrepancy

A supplementary aggregate-only diagnostic reconstructs common-cohort subgroup moments from archived pooled moments and bounded optima. A three-factor Shapley decomposition of the 2023 annual-minus-common H difference assigns 0.000662720 of 0.000683500 to differences in alignment per unit correction energy (A/E), versus 0.000032262 to group proportions and -0.000011482 to correction energy. The six seed/profile sensitivities retain alignment-per-energy dominance. This is an arithmetic comparison of observed residual distributions with hypothetical moment hybrids, not causal selection, a correlation analysis, or new uncertainty. The zero-history primary remains under-supported. [Supplementary mechanics](INFORMATION_HETEROGENEITY_MEMBERSHIP_MECHANICS_20261126.md).

## Publication decision

Keep one main empirical paper on managed allocation (17 completed analyses), expand its discussion with the separate rank, GPS and parental-observation evidence, and keep a traceable repository-wide program and separate papers for GPS/phenotype, rank temporal transport, pedigree/genomic infrastructure and collective market representation. Neither combining incompatible response units nor appending unrelated positive metrics would strengthen the core. A larger single integrated biological mechanism paper needs same-horse joins, authorized data, new validation and measured observer behavior.
